If you're considering buying a home in El Paso, Texas, you may have heard one concern come up repeatedly:

**“Are property taxes really that high in El Paso?”**

Texas is known for not having an individual state income tax, but property taxes are an important part of the cost of owning a home.

For El Paso buyers, understanding property taxes before making an offer is especially important because the home's purchase price isn't the only number that determines your monthly housing budget.

Property taxes can affect your total monthly payment, your escrow account, and ultimately how much home you're comfortable buying.

Here's what you should know.

## How Do Property Taxes Work in El Paso, Texas?

Property taxes in Texas are local taxes.

Instead of one single statewide property tax rate, different local taxing units can impose property taxes. Depending on the property, these can include entities such as the county, city, school district, community college district, hospital district, and other applicable taxing units.

Your annual property tax bill is generally determined by two major components:

- The property's taxable value
- The applicable tax rates from the property's taxing units

Because of this, two homes with similar listing prices can potentially have different property tax bills.

## Who Determines the Value of a Home for Property Taxes?

In El Paso County, property values for taxation purposes are appraised by the **El Paso Central Appraisal District (EPCAD)**.

The appraisal district determines property values, while local taxing units establish their tax rates.

This distinction is important.

The appraisal district does not simply decide how much tax you owe. It establishes values used in the property tax process, while the applicable taxing entities determine their respective rates.

## Is the Appraised Value the Same as the Listing Price?

Not necessarily.

The price advertised on a real estate listing, the amount a buyer ultimately pays, and the property's appraisal-district value are different concepts.

A home's listing price is determined as part of the real estate market.

The appraisal district establishes a value for property-tax purposes according to Texas property-tax rules.

Because these numbers serve different purposes, buyers should never assume that the taxes shown for a previous year will remain exactly the same after purchasing the property.

## Why Can Two $300,000 Homes Have Different Property Taxes?

Imagine you're comparing two El Paso homes that are both listed for $300,000.

Even though the asking prices are identical, their property-tax situations may differ.

Factors can include:

- Taxing jurisdictions
- Taxable value
- Applicable exemptions
- Previous ownership circumstances
- Property characteristics
- Changes in appraised value

This is one reason buyers should evaluate each property individually rather than applying one generic percentage to every El Paso home.

## What Is a Homestead Exemption in Texas?

For qualifying homeowners, a residence homestead exemption can reduce the taxable value used by certain taxing units.

Texas provides a mandatory school-district residence homestead exemption, and other exemptions may also apply depending on the homeowner and taxing jurisdiction.

Eligibility requirements matter, so buyers should verify current exemption rules rather than assuming that an exemption attached to the previous owner's tax bill will automatically apply to them.

## Does the Homestead Exemption Make Property Taxes Disappear?

No.

A homestead exemption can reduce taxable value for applicable taxing units, but it doesn't normally eliminate the entire property tax bill.

Its effect depends on the exemptions for which the homeowner qualifies and the taxing units associated with the property.

For eligible homeowners, however, filing for the appropriate exemptions can be an important part of managing long-term homeownership costs.

## What Is the Texas Homestead Appraisal Cap?

Texas also provides an appraisal limitation for qualifying residence homesteads.

Once applicable, the limitation generally restricts how much the appraised value used for property-tax purposes can increase from one year to the next under the state's rules.

However, buyers should be careful when looking at a seller's existing tax history.

A previous owner's exemptions and appraisal limitations may not represent what a new owner will experience after purchasing the property.

That's why historical property taxes should be treated as useful information, not necessarily a guarantee of future taxes.

## Can Property Taxes Change After I Buy a House?

Yes.

Property taxes can change over time for several reasons.

Potential factors include:

- Changes in property value
- Changes in tax rates
- Changes in exemptions
- New improvements to the property
- Changes in applicable taxing jurisdictions or assessments
- Ownership changes

This is particularly important when purchasing a home that has been owned by the same person for many years.

The previous owner's tax bill may reflect circumstances that won't necessarily apply to the new buyer.

## Can Property Taxes Make My Mortgage Payment Go Up?

Yes, potentially.

If your mortgage payment includes an escrow account for property taxes and homeowners insurance, changes in those expenses can affect the amount your lender needs to collect.

For example, if the lender determines that more money will be needed to cover property taxes, the escrow portion of your monthly mortgage payment may increase.

This can surprise homeowners who have a fixed-rate mortgage.

The interest rate may be fixed, but that doesn't necessarily mean the total amount paid each month can never change.

## Why Did My Mortgage Payment Increase If I Have a Fixed Rate?

This is a common source of confusion.

With a fixed-rate mortgage, the principal-and-interest portion of the payment is generally fixed according to the loan terms.

However, the total monthly payment may also include:

- Property taxes
- Homeowners insurance
- Mortgage insurance, when applicable
- Other escrowed expenses

If taxes or insurance change, the total amount collected each month can change even though the mortgage interest rate itself remains fixed.

## How Can I Check Property Taxes Before Buying a Home?

Before purchasing an El Paso property, buyers should review the property's available tax information rather than relying only on the listing.

Useful information to investigate can include:

- Current appraised value
- Current taxable value
- Previous tax bills
- Existing exemptions
- Taxing jurisdictions
- Current applicable tax rates

The **El Paso Central Appraisal District** provides property information that can help buyers research individual properties.

Your Realtor, lender, title company, and appropriate tax professionals can also help you understand how property taxes may affect a specific transaction.

## Should I Use the Seller's Current Tax Bill to Calculate My Payment?

Use it as a reference, but don't assume your future tax bill will be identical.

The seller may have exemptions, appraisal limitations, or other circumstances that don't apply to you.

This is particularly important when comparing estimated mortgage payments online.

A calculator may show an attractive principal-and-interest payment while underestimating taxes, insurance, or other housing expenses.

Before deciding whether a home fits your budget, look at the complete estimated monthly housing cost.

## What Should I Budget for Besides Property Taxes?

Property taxes are only one component of homeownership.

A more complete El Paso housing budget should consider:

- Principal and interest
- Property taxes
- Homeowners insurance
- Mortgage insurance, when applicable
- HOA fees, when applicable
- Electricity
- Water
- Gas
- Internet
- Maintenance and repairs

This is why the purchase price alone doesn't tell you whether a home is affordable.

## Are Property Taxes More Important Than the Home Price?

Both matter.

Suppose you're comparing two homes with similar prices but different estimated taxes, insurance expenses, HOA fees, and energy costs.

Their monthly cost of ownership could be noticeably different even though their listing prices are almost identical.

Instead of focusing exclusively on:

**“How much does this house cost?”**

Ask:

**“Approximately how much will this house cost me each month?”**

That provides a much more useful framework for comparing properties.

## Can I Protest My Property Appraisal in El Paso?

Texas property owners generally have rights to protest certain appraisal-district actions, including issues involving property value.

There are specific procedures and deadlines involved.

If you receive a Notice of Appraised Value and believe there is an issue with the appraisal, review the current protest information provided by the El Paso Central Appraisal District and the Texas Comptroller.

Property owners should pay close attention to the deadline shown on their notice rather than relying on a date from a previous year.

## What Should Homebuyers Ask Before Making an Offer?

Before making an offer on an El Paso home, consider asking:

- What were the property's previous taxes?
- What is the current appraised value?
- What is the current taxable value?
- What exemptions does the current owner have?
- Which taxing entities apply to this address?
- Are there HOA fees?
- What might homeowners insurance cost?
- What are the home's typical utility expenses?

These questions can help you understand the property beyond its listing price.

## So, Are Property Taxes Really That High in El Paso?

Property taxes are an important expense for El Paso homeowners, but there isn't one tax amount that applies to every property.

Your actual tax obligation depends on the individual property's taxable value, applicable tax rates, taxing jurisdictions, exemptions, and other circumstances.

For buyers, the most important lesson is simple:

**Don't evaluate a home using the mortgage principal and interest alone.**

Look at the complete cost of ownership before deciding how much house fits your budget.
