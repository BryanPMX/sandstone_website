# 3 Mistakes That Destroy Your Credit Before Buying a Home

Buying a home is one of the biggest financial decisions you'll ever make — and your credit score plays a bigger role in it than most people realize. A few points can be the difference between qualifying for a great mortgage rate and getting denied altogether.

The problem? Most credit mistakes happen right before buyers apply for a loan, often without them even knowing it. Here are the three most common ones — and how to avoid them.

## 1. Opening New Credit Accounts Too Soon

It's tempting to open a new credit card for that "0% financing" deal on furniture for your future home, or to finance a new car right before house hunting. But every new credit application triggers a hard inquiry, which can temporarily lower your score — and lenders will notice the new debt on your report.

#### What to do instead:

Avoid opening any new credit accounts for at least 6 months before applying for a mortgage.
If you need something for the new house, wait until after closing.
Keep your existing accounts open and in good standing. 

## 2. Maxing Out or Overusing Credit Cards

Your credit utilization ratio — how much of your available credit you're using — makes up a large portion of your credit score. Buyers often rack up balances on moving expenses, new furniture, or renovations before they've even closed on the home.

### What to do instead:

Keep credit card balances below 30% of your limit (ideally under 10% for the best scores).
Pay down existing balances before applying for pre-approval.
Avoid large purchases on credit until after your loan has funded. 

## 3. Missing or Making Late Payments

Even one missed payment can significantly damage your credit score and stay on your report for years. Lenders look closely at your payment history in the months leading up to your mortgage application — a single late payment can raise red flags or even delay closing.

### What to do instead:

Set up autopay or reminders for every bill, not just credit cards (utilities and phone bills matter too).
Double-check your credit report for errors before applying.
If you're unsure about your current standing, request a free credit report and review it carefully.


## The Bottom Line

Your credit score isn't just a number — it directly affects your interest rate, your loan options, and how much home you can afford. Avoiding these three mistakes in the months before you apply can save you thousands of dollars over the life of your loan.
